About mouurn
mouurn exists for the moments when loss arrives and you don't know what to do next.

We believe grief deserves more than a casserole and an awkward silence. More than "let me know if you need anything." More than three to five days of bereavement leave.

What we believe

Grief is not a problem to be solved or a phase to move through as quickly as possible. It is not something to manage quietly so the people around you are more comfortable. It is one of the most human experiences there is, and it deserves to be tended.

Grief is also complicated. It doesn't arrive neatly, and it doesn't require a particular kind of relationship. There are no comparisons here. There are so many different kinds of loss — and they all deserve to be honored.

For centuries, communities held their grieving members. There were rituals, timelines, gatherings — visible markers that said: this person is in mourning, be gentle with them. Somewhere along the way, we stopped. We got efficient. We got busy. We got uncomfortable with death.

mouurn is trying to bring some of that back. Not the black armbands necessarily. But the permission. The acknowledgment. The practical and emotional infrastructure to hold people when loss arrives — which it always does.

The name
mouurn
from Old English · murnan · to care deeply, to tend, to grieve

Mourning comes from the Old English murnan — to care deeply, to be troubled, to grieve. It is not just about loss. It is about the act of caring so much that you have to stop and tend to it.

We named it mouurn to slow that word down. The double u is intentional — it makes you linger. It is the sound of taking time. Of not rushing through what matters.

Mourning is also ritual. For centuries, people marked their grief visibly — with gathered community, with time set apart, with the world knowing to be gentle. We are bringing that back. Not as obligation, but as permission.

Our founder
Natasha Sizlo
Founder · Author · Grief Coach

Natasha Sizlo knows grief from the inside.

She is the author of All Signs Point to Paris: A Memoir of Love, Loss and Destiny (Mariner Books / Sugar23 Books), a certified grief guide, Forest and Ocean Therapy practitioner, and one of the most trusted voices working in loss today. She has sat with hundreds of grievers — in private practice, in retreats, in the wild — and what she heard, again and again, was the same thing: people are good. They show up with meals and open hearts and the best intentions. And then the world moves on, long before the grief does. The griever is left not knowing how to ask for what they need, and the people who love them not knowing how to offer it.

mouurn is her answer to that gap. Built from her own losses — her father, her home in the fires, and the particular loneliness of grief that outlasts everyone's attention — it is the guide she wished had existed. For the griever. For the people who love them. For all of it.

Welcome to mouurn."

I
Grief is not linear.

There are no five stages. There is no timeline. There is only your loss, your life, and the people around you. We meet you wherever you are.

II
Every loss counts.

A death, a divorce, a diagnosis, a pet, a pregnancy, a friendship, a job. No loss is too small for a ritual, and no grief is too complicated to hold.

III
Community is medicine.

Grief is not meant to be carried alone. Everything we build exists to make it easier for people to show up for each other.

Who mouurn is for
Every loss deserves to be held.

No comparison. No hierarchy. Every kind of loss, every kind of griever.

The griever

In the thick of it. Needing practical help, a plan, and people to show up.

The helper

The sister, the best friend, the coworker who wants to do something real.

The anticipatory

Someone whose person is still here, but not for long. Planning ahead with love.

The long-term

Two years out. Five years out. Still carrying it. Still deserving of a place.

The divorced

A marriage ending is a loss. It deserves the same tenderness and ritual.

The diagnosed

A diagnosis is a before and after. We're here for the hard, uncertain middle.

The complicated

Grief doesn't require a good relationship. It only requires a loss. You belong here.

The pet lover

Their life mattered. Your grief matters. Full stop.
